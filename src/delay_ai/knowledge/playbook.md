# Planning playbook

Domain rules the planner follows when proposing ways to recover a delay. Edit
this file to add the contractor's own experience.

## Sequencing
- MEP first fix, drylining and fit-out need the building watertight. Don't
  propose starting them before the envelope and roof are complete, unless the
  option says how floors will be temporarily weathertight.
- The facade (curtain walling, windows) can follow the frame floor by floor.
  Overlapping it with the upper floor slabs is a real option.
- Lift installation needs the shaft complete; commissioning needs permanent power.
- External works can usually move freely; they're rarely critical.
- Never remove a dependency that protects safety: edge protection before work
  at height, temporary works before loading, CDM 2015 duties.

## Acceleration
- Labour-driven activities (erection, drylining, fit-out) can usually be cut by
  up to 30% with an extra gang, longer shifts or weekend working, at a cost.
- Curing, drying and testing times can't meaningfully be compressed.
- Acceleration only helps on the critical path; check float first.

## Deliveries
- When an order is late, the supplier has just told you their earliest date.
  Don't propose asking them to bring that same order forward: recover the time
  elsewhere in the programme.
- For orders that aren't late yet, phased deliveries help: steel by erection
  sequence, kitchens and bathrooms floor by floor. Work can start on the first
  phase.
- An alternative supplier is worth asking about for standard items (plasterboard,
  sanitaryware, flooring), rarely for fabricated or bespoke items (steel,
  curtain walling, lifts).
- Expediting costs money and isn't certain: say so in the risks.

## Options
- Offer genuinely different strategies, e.g. one supply-side, one sequencing,
  one acceleration, and say which trades and subcontractors are affected.
